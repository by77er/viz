## Blocker

### B-1. During a deploy, an id-range reader misses a series and the check that should catch it is silent (D59)

D59's check works only if *every* reader counts the series whose id is in its range and whose
key is another part's (`foreign`). The coordinator checks `foreign > 0` summed over every reader
(`coordinator.ts:1259`, `:1321`). A reader of the previous release (ABI 4) ignores
`seriesByPart`, reads the series files by name and time, and returns no `foreign` field. The
split leaf adds `answer.foreign ?? 0` (`leaf.ts:538`), so the old reader counts as "saw none".
The comment at `leaf.ts:109–110` says this is safe: "(and says nothing: none is foreign then)".
That is true of the old reader's own part, but not of the parts its count is supposed to
vouch for. A series S with `__type__` whose **id** is in part A and whose **key** is in part B is
seen only by part A's reader. If that reader is old, nobody reports S. Part B's reader, of this
release, reads by B's id range and never sees S. The split answers 200 without S.

COMPATIBILITY.md §1.1 to §1.3 say this mix happens on every deploy and rollback: Durable Objects
run one version each, readers are other leaf objects picked by hash (`leaf.ts:514`), and old
objects keep serving for up to 30 s or longer. The promise in that contract's preamble is
"must fail *closed*".

**Reproduced (R-6).** One `rv_dep_total` series carries `__type__="counter"` and 8 do not. It is
flushed to the lake and its tail is discarded. At L = 4 the typed series' id is in part 0 and
its key is in part 1. Part 1's id range lies outside the series file's `sid` bounds, so its
reader skips the file. The readers serving part 0 are made to behave as ABI 4: a prototype
wrapper drops `seriesByPart` from the request and `foreign` from the answer. The rest of the
code is unmodified.

- Control, every reader of this release: `X-Viz-Series-Reads: id-range,name-time`. The body is
  identical to one place: `sum by (job)(rate(rv_dep_total[1m]))` = 9.0667.
- Attack: **200**, `id-range`, value **2.4000**. The typed series' rate is missing, and nothing
  says so.

**Required before merge.** An answer that does not say it honoured `seriesByPart` has to count
as "foreign unknown". For example: readers of ABI 5 always return `foreign` as a number; the
split leaf returns `foreignUnknown` when any reader's answer lacked it; and the coordinator
runs the split again by name and time when any part reports foreign or unknown. Add R-6 as a
test. It has teeth: it fails on the current code. Also correct `leaf.ts:109–110`, D59's
"Decision", and the README line "the check is exact because every such series is seen by the
reader of the part its id is in". That line holds only when every reader is ABI 5.

## Majors

### M-1. When the coordinator never learns the tail's end, the parts answer from different snapshots (D54)

If the coordinator's first page of a shard is `page_limit`, `through` stays null (`tail.ts:154`,
`:170–175`). Each part then reads "to the shard's end" whenever it is called. D54 records this
under "Cost of it" ("a part may see an append others do not"). It is more than a cost: the
query returns, with status 200, a value that no state of the store ever held. One scrape is
half visible. Prometheus' reads ignore "all appends above the max, or that are incomplete"
(`tsdb/isolation.go`), so a Prometheus query sees a scrape whole or not at all. AGENTS.md:
"refuse rather than guess".

This is not rare. In one place the coordinator's cap is `maxFetchBytes − lakeBytes`
(`coordinator.ts:913`), so any query whose lake bytes nearly fill 16 MiB reads its tails with a
cap of a few bytes, and every shard's `through` is null.

**Reproduced (R-1).** 40 series of one sender, two scrapes of value 1, and `sum(rv_g)`
instant. Split over 2 parts, one after another (`sequentialLeaves`). One scrape of every series
(a new value) is written between part 0's call and part 1's.

| Coordinator's first page | Before | After | Split answered |
|---|---|---|---|
| fitted (`through` known) | 40 | 80 | **40** (consistent: the snapshot the coordinator saw) |
| over the cap (`through` null) | 80 | 120 | **110** (20 series old, 20 new) |

The mutant "a paged rest is not bounded by `through`" (`tail.ts:158` deleted) makes the first row
answer 70, and this test catches it.

**Required.** Learn the end even when the first page does not fit, and then bound every part
by it. Options: `page_limit` carries `through`, which is additive in `v` 1 (contracts first); or
the coordinator calls `Shard.describe(tenant)` and uses `nextSeq − 1`; or each part returns the
`through` it read to and the coordinator refuses (503, retryable) when they differ. This also
fixes m-3's estimate.

### M-2. The coordinator and a part hold the byte cap once per shard, and the split path holds the tail twice (D54)

- `readTails` gives each shard its own `{cap: limit, used: 0}` (`coordinator.ts:481`).
  `readRests` gives each shard `{cap, used: sent}` (`tail.ts:370`), and checks the sum only after
  every read has finished (`:383`). Peak memory is S × cap, not cap. **Reproduced (R-3)**, with
  two shards. The coordinator received **78,944–92,128 bytes** with a cap of 47,390 before
  refusing, 1.7–1.9× the cap. One part received **52,624** with a cap of 31,593. With the
  default `maxFetchBytes` (16 MiB) and spread 2 that is 32 MiB of tail. A dedicated pool with a
  larger spread multiplies it.
- On the over path, `cutAll` copies what was read (`:770`). The closure `tailFor` keeps `reads`
  alive to name the shards (`:773`), and `gather` keeps `tails`. Both copies live for the whole
  split, and again for D59's rerun: about 2 × S × cap of JavaScript heap on a **success** path.
  The JavaScript heap is not in `X-Viz-Wasm-Memory-Bytes`, the only figure D54 reports
  ("2.0 MiB of coordinator WASM").
- A tail that fits (≤ `maxFetchBytes`) is still cut in the engine at the coordinator (`:759`).
  **Measured here (M-1, local workerd):** a 5.09 MiB tail of 20,000 series was cut in the engine
  with **31.6 MiB** of coordinator WASM (6.2×). The JavaScript cut of the same tail took
  **1.6 MiB**, and both bodies were identical to one place. D54's own figure is 132.6 MiB for
  21.17 MiB (6.3×). Extrapolated linearly (modelled), a tail just under 16 MiB cut in the engine
  needs about 95–105 MiB of WASM, plus the batches and the parts in the JavaScript heap. That is
  past a 128 MB isolate on the path D54 kept as the safe one. `ISOLATE_BUDGET_BYTES` counts lake
  bytes only, so concurrent queries are not held back by it. **Experiment that settles it:**
  `tail.bench.ts` dataset with the tail sized to 15 MiB and `forceLeaves`, reporting peak WASM
  plus `process.memoryUsage`-equivalent heap from workerd.

**Required.** One budget across shards: for example `cap / S` each, or a shared budget with the
order-dependence accepted and stated. Drop the references to `reads` once the batches are cut.
Use the engine cut only below a measured threshold (for example 4 MiB) and the JavaScript cut
above it.

### M-3. A split over a tail that does not fit scans each shard's whole tail L + 1 times, on the object that acknowledges writes (D54)

D48 measured that a filtered read costs a scan of the tenant's whole tail on the shard, whatever
it returns: 55–86 ms for one split leaf's key range of a 22 MB tail (`shard/README.md`, local).
D54 sends a scan from the coordinator plus one from every part (up to `maxLeaves` = 32), and
only when the tail is over 16 MiB, which is exactly when scans are expensive. Modelled from those
local figures: about 1.8–2.8 s of one shard's single thread per query at L = 32 on a 22 MB tail,
and more as the tail grows. That is in calls of up to `tailScanMaxBytes` (32 MiB, about 100 ms
each) during which appends wait. Shards are pooled across tenants (D13), so one tenant's
dashboard queries delay other tenants' ingest. That can reach D37's early refusals (503 to
senders). D48 names "Filtered reads show on the shard's CPU under load" as the condition that
would change it; D54 multiplies the number of those reads by L without measuring it.
**Experiment that settles it:** on Cloudflare, appends at a steady rate to one shard while split
queries over a 20–50 MiB tail run at L = 8 and 32; measure append latency and refusals. Until
then, cap L × scanned bytes per shard per query (refuse beyond it) or run the parts' rests
one shard-call at a time.

## Minors

### m-1. Appends after `through` count against a part's page budget; a query whose tail fits is refused (D54) — reproduced (R-2)

The leaf filters batches past the coordinator's `through` only after the shard has returned
them (`tail.ts:158`). The shard still counts them in the page, and a `page_limit` on one of them
sets `complete = false` (`:170–175`) → `too_large` → **422** non-retryable. The message also
overstates the tail. In R-2 a part's cap was 24,384 bytes. The control without an append
answered 200, identical to one place. With one large scrape appended after the coordinator's
read, the answer was **422** "the unflushed tail of one of 2 parts is at least **302,128**
bytes", counting rows that would have been dropped. Rare with default caps; also wasted shard
scans past `through`. Fix: stop at `through` (a `page_limit` with `seq > through` means the read
is complete), or pass `through` to the shard (contract).

### m-2. A shard rolled back between the coordinator's read and a part's: the whole unfiltered rest is counted against the part's cap (D54) — reproduced (R-5)

An unpaged answer to a part is every series and metric above `above`. `readRests` counts all of
it against the part's cap before the part cuts out its own share (`tail.ts:383`; the cut is in
`leaf.ts:493–498`). With a cap of 24,384 bytes and another metric in the tail, the same query
answered 200 with filtering and **422** "at least 246,224 bytes" when the shard answered the part
as the previous release does. A transient release state gets a non-retryable error. Count the
part's cut, or answer 503.

### m-3. A split sized from a read that saw no page is too small, and is refused instead of re-split (D54) — reproduced (R-7)

With `through` null, `tailEstimate` adds nothing for the rest (`tail.ts:315`): the estimate is one
batch. L came out as 2, and each part was over its cap: **422** "one of 2 parts is at least
158,976 bytes, the limit is 140,136". The same query with 8 leaves answered **200**, identical to
one place. D54 mentions only the series estimate as a cause. M-1's fix (know the end) fixes the
estimate; otherwise re-split once on `too_large` (D31 alternative (d)).

### m-4. D59's tests show that the rerun happens, never that it is needed (D59)

Mutant "readers never report foreign" (`foreign: ex.foreign` → `foreign: 0` at `leaf.ts:429`,
`:462`) is killed in the tree only by `expect(reread).toBeGreaterThan(0)`, a header count.
Every body in that test stays correct, because each test series file is one row group whose
`sid` bounds intersect every part's range. My two randomized D59 differentials (11 part counts ×
3 queries, and 8 fresh tenants × 7 part counts with few series) also passed under that mutant.
`query/README.md:849–851` says a reader that ignores `seriesByPart` is something "no query-suite
world can tell apart". R-6 shows it can: with one row group, choose L so that the stray's key
part lies outside the file's `sid` bounds. Adopt R-6's construction (with the B-1 fix) so that a
missing check produces a wrong body.

### m-5. Label calls over leaves: the query-wide byte limit counts one copy of the series files while L parts read them, and the one-place `maxFiles` still applies (D56)

`coordinator.ts:1031–1034` checks `maxFiles` (2,000, a one-place limit) before choosing leaves,
and `maxQueryFetchBytes` (512 MiB) against one copy of the series files. A `/labels` call without
`match[]` pins no name, so `partPlan` cannot prune by id and every part reads every row group.
Up to 32 × 512 MiB can be fetched and decoded for one call. D56 records the L-fold reads as a
cost. The limit should count them, and over leaves `maxQueryFiles` is the limit that applies.

### m-6. `maxMetadataSeries` refuses before `limit` shortens (D56)

Prometheus never refuses `/labels`, label values or `/series` for size. With `limit`, it returns
the first `limit` items and the warning (`api.go`). viz answers 422 above 200,000 series even
when `limit=10`. D56 alternative (e) records the choice, with "would change if Prometheus
clients come to depend on limit shortening the work". Grafana's Prometheus data source sends
`limit` on these calls for that purpose (training knowledge, not verified here), so that
condition may already hold. At least name it in `query/README.md` "Not implemented".

### m-7. A paged read has no deadline or progress check between pages (D54)

`readPages` loops until `next` is null (`tail.ts:113–181`) without `checkDeadline`. A shard
that answers the same cursor with nothing (for example a `tailScanMaxBytes` of 0) spins until the
CPU limit, and a long tail paged at 32 MiB a call runs past `timeoutMs` before the deadline is
checked again. Check the deadline per page, and refuse a page whose cursor did not advance.

## Nits

- **n-1** (pre-existing, also on `main`): `/labels` and label values accept a `match[]` whose
  matchers all match the empty string (`{a=""}`, `{a=~".*"}`). Prometheus answers `bad_data`
  "match[] must contain at least one non-empty matcher" (`parseMatchersParam`, used by all
  three endpoints). viz checks this only for `/series`.
- **n-2:** `/series` objects with index-like label names (`"9"`, `"10"`) come out in JavaScript
  key order (`"9"` first) in one place, over leaves and with `limit`. Byte identity across the
  three holds (R-4). Prometheus emits label order (`"10"` first). The JSON is equal either way.
- **n-3:** `contracts/TABLES.md` "Reading part of a file" allows `metric_series` files to be read
  "by name" only. D59 reads them by `series_id` range. The rule is the same as for sample
  files, but the contract text should say so (contracts first).
- **n-4:** the cardinality report's leaf retry `a = await call(1)` (`coordinator.ts:1432`) is not
  wrapped. A second failure becomes the generic 503 "a storage component did not answer", not
  a message naming the leaf, unlike the other two leaf calls.

## What was attacked and held

- **Filter pushdown (D54), held, with teeth.** 24 PromQL shapes the workload lacks × 4
  evaluation grids (aligned range, a 37 s step offset by 7 s, instant, instant 1 ms off a
  sample) × 2 tenants = 192 queries, each compared byte for byte across three paths: the
  previous-release shard (unfiltered, unpaged), this release (filtered, paged), and split with
  the rest read by parts (cap 1 over 2 leaves, half the tail over 3; 44–52 went through
  `+leaves`). No difference in any of them. The shapes were:
  - `{nonexistent=""}` (absent label);
  - `or` with no shared pin, and a shared `job="web"` over two metrics;
  - `absent(…{job="nope"})` and `absent_over_time`;
  - `last_over_time(up[1ms])` and `count_over_time(up[15s])` at the hull's edges;
  - negative `offset`, `@ start()`, and `@ end() offset`;
  - subqueries with `offset` and with `@`;
  - `timestamp()` and `timestamp(… @ t)`;
  - `{__name__="up", job="web"}`, `unless` between names, and a `__name__` regex with a pinned
    label;
  - `present_over_time`, `quantile_over_time`, `topk by`, and the staleness window.

  Mutants on a scratch copy: the label filter taken from the first range only — killed
  (`or-no-shared`, and `/labels` with two `match[]`); the hull shrunk by 1 ms — killed
  (`last-1ms`). By construction the filter is a superset of the merged plan ranges (`plan.rs`
  `plan_is_sound`), and the shard's `''` means "absent", as PromQL's `=""` does.
- **Label endpoints (D56), held.** 7 calls × 4 windows × 2 tenants × 4 paths = 224, all
  byte-identical to the unfiltered one-place answer. The calls were names, values and
  `/series`, with one or two `match[]`, `{nonexistent=""}`, a regex name with a pinned label, and
  `limit`. The windows were `[t, t]` on a sample, `[t+1, t+14999]`, `[t−1, t−1]`, and unbounded.
  The paths were filtered, over 3 leaves, the rest read by parts at cap 1 over 2, and at a
  quarter of the tail over 3. Tail rows are "in the range" per row, in the engine
  (`meta.rs` `listed_as`) and in the shard filter alike. `limit` follows `api.go` exactly:
  `len > limit` truncates and warns, `0` disables, a negative value is "limit must be
  non-negative". Order is deterministic: code-point order equals UTF-8 byte order
  (`meta.ts`), and every `query_range` matrix of the workload was in label order, so
  `limit` keeps what Prometheus keeps.
- **D59 with every reader of this release, held.** 33 + 56 split runs (L from 2 to 12; 65 of
  them re-ran by name and time) were identical to one place. The mechanism is sound when every
  reader reports. `part_sids` handles the signed wrap. `may_hold` follows the
  `(metric_name, series_id)` sort order, which `encode.rs` writes in signed `i64` order.
- **Part bounds, held.** `splitKeyRange`, `cutById`, `part_of` (`u128` shift) and `part_sids` all
  use `ceil(j·2^64/L)`. The tree's edge-id test and my split runs (the leaf's strays check would
  refuse on any disagreement; none did) agree.
- **Stale cursors, held** (by reading, plus the tree's tests). Every page re-checks incarnation
  and discard fence. A leaf's rest is fenced at the coordinator's last kept `seq`. A flush
  recorded at or below it changes nothing, because the lake snapshot is older. Above it the
  answer is `stale` and the query re-plans. A previous-release answer in the middle of a paged
  read is a 503.
- **Release skew, held** apart from B-1, m-1 and m-2. An old shard answers whole and is used. An
  old leaf given `tailParts` → 503. An old coordinator never sends options. An old split leaf
  drops `seriesByPart`, so its readers read by name.
- **Six connections, held.** Every R2 read goes through `inWaves(…, 6)` or a wave of six, and
  `one()` holds one connection at a time. Shard and leaf calls are Durable Object RPC, not
  subrequests in the platform's list.
- **R2 cache by byte range, held.** Keys are content-hashed and carry the tenant's path, and
  ranges add `offset`/`length` to the key. A different L gives different ranges, which lowers the
  hit rate; it is never stale.
- **Tenant isolation, held.** The tenant comes only from the API key and reaches the leaf in the
  request. `LEAF` is bound only in `viz-query` (`query/wrangler.jsonc`;
  `deploy/generated/stage-citest/viz-query.jsonc`). Every shard SQL statement names the tenant,
  and the cursor is bound to it. Both tenants of each test world share every shard object, and
  each answered exactly its own unfiltered answer.

## Verdicts

- **D54 (filtered, paged tail; parts read the rest): approve with changes.** The filter and the
  paging are correct. Required before merge: M-1 (know the end or refuse) and M-2 (one budget
  across shards, release the reads after cutting, a measured threshold for the engine cut). M-3
  needs its experiment, or a bound on L × scanned bytes, before large tails go through the
  parts. m-1 to m-3 are small and recommended.
- **D56 (label endpoints over leaves, `limit`): approve.** Byte-identical in every case tried;
  `limit` matches Prometheus' code. Recommended: m-5, m-6, n-1.
- **D59 (series files by id range, foreign check): reject until B-1 is fixed.** The fix is a few
  lines. Adopt R-6, which fails today, as its test (m-4).
