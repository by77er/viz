A time-series chart: the product's core. `vz-chart` inside a `vz-panel-body`: an SVG plot (`vz-plot`) drawn by script at the pixel size of its region, a foot (`vz-chart-foot`) with the readout and zoom, the legend as a table of series (`vz-legend-table`) and the legend note. The preview is a working reference implementation in plain script and SVG (`dist/viz-chart.js` is built from it); its functions (`window.vzChartRules`: `assignSeries`, `chartModel`, `drawnSet`, `chartFacts`, `otherNote`, `textLines`, `distinguish`, `fit`, `scaleUnit`, `valueTicks`, `numberFormat`) are the rules below as code, pure (no page), and `tools/test/chart-rules.test.mjs` checks them as properties over random sequences of results, filters, query edits and picks. Every case below is in the preview.

Every colour, stroke width, dash and type size comes from the classes in `components.css`. A chart implementation sets geometry only. Canvas implementations read the same tokens.

## Series colour

1. **Slots.** `series-1` to `series-8`, in that order, never shuffled. Odd slots are the darker step and even slots the lighter, so neighbours differ by at least 1.3:1 in luminance (greyscale, and every kind of colour vision). **Slots 1 to 4 are drawn solid and 5 to 8 dashed** (`space-2` dash, `space-1` gap): within each group of four every pair passes the colour-vision validator (all pairs, because lines cross and any two can meet: light worst 10.6 under simulated colour blindness and 21.1 for normal vision, dark 9.7 and 18.7); between the groups the hue and lightness steps repeat, and the dash tells them apart. A slot never looks like a status or action colour (8 or more apart in OKLab). Every slot reaches 3:1 on `ground`, `raised`, `sunken`, `hover` and `rule` in both themes; on `pressed` (as long as a finger is down) the legend swatch is carried by its own `ground` ring. `check.mjs` checks all of this, and renders it.
2. **A view owns a map from series to slot.** A series is identified by its full label set. The map lives as long as the view: an Explore tab keeps it for the tab's session (it survives reloads; it is discarded when the tab closes); a shared link carries the ordered list of up to eight series keys, so the recipient opens with the sender's colours. **A dashboard panel stores its map with the panel**, written only when someone with edit rights saves the panel; each viewer's copy starts from the stored map, and a viewer's filter, range, query edit or recolour changes that copy for the tab's session and never the stored map. Series in the stored map open in the same colours for every viewer. **A series that appeared after the last save is not in the stored map**: each viewer's copy places it by the algorithm below the first time that viewer sees it, so two viewers can see it in different colours until someone with edit rights saves the panel again, which stores it.
3. **The algorithm: what a person sees wins.** (v8. v7 recorded the project lead's decision and applied it to filters only; the v7 review found a query narrowed in Explore leaving eleven pods grey beside seven colours kept for pods that had left. The lead's rule, as it now reads: a series that is not in the result is not on screen either, so its slot is open exactly as a hidden series' is. This replaces v7's rule 3, which kept such a slot reserved, and v6's, under which a hidden series also kept its slot.) A series is **on screen** when it is in the result and the view's filter does not hide it; **hidden** when the view's own filter hides it (a filter in the chart, or a label matcher a dashboard filter adds to the query); **away** when it is in neither (a query edited or narrowed, a pod gone). To the colours, hidden and away are the same: neither is on screen. The map records, for every series that has been on screen, the slot it had the last time it was on screen (1 to 8, or Other) and the last step at which it held it; and, for each slot, the series it belongs to now, its *owner*. A slot is **held** when its owner is on screen; otherwise it is **open** (**free** if no series has had it since the map began). On each step (a new result, or a change of the filter), in this order:
   1. Series on screen now and at the step before keep their slot, or Other. A colour on screen never changes.
   2. Series that come on screen (back from hidden or from away) take back the slot they had, if no series on screen owns it. When two want one slot, the one that held it more recently takes it.
   3. Then every series on screen in Other (new, back without its slot, or there already), in order of rank (the series' highest value in the window, largest first; ties by full label set), takes an open slot while one is left: a free one first, else the one whose owner left the screen longest ago (ties: the lowest slot).
   4. Series on screen record the step with their slot, or record Other.

   So **the largest series on screen is never grey while a slot is open**, whichever way the screen changed: a query edited or narrowed, a filter applied or cleared, a round trip, a pod gone. Hidden and away series are never given a slot; they keep theirs until rule 3 gives it to a series on screen. **Choosing legend rows changes what is drawn, not what is on screen**: an unchosen row stays listed, with its colour, so a toggle never changes a colour. Clearing a filter or editing a query back is a step like any other.

   Consequences, each a case in the preview and a test in `tools/test/chart-rules.test.mjs`: on first load the eight largest are coloured, by size; a series on screen keeps its colour while it stays on screen; the same narrowing gives the same colours whether it is a query edit or a dashboard filter (the v7 review's image 03: the eight largest pods on node-b are coloured either way; a property over 300 random sequences of narrowing and widening done both ways); a query edited to series it does not share and edited back returns every series to its colour, because the other query's largest take the open slots and then leave the screen (v5 review, image 02); with eight or fewer series on screen nothing needs an open slot, so a filter, a narrowing or a pod that misses a result changes no colour; with more, the largest series in Other take the open slots at once, and a series coming back finds its slot held on screen and joins Other, where the note says it is larger than a coloured series. **"Colour the eight largest" is offered only when pressing it would change a colour**: when one of the eight largest series on screen is not coloured (rule 5; v9).
4. **Open slots and churn.** A slot whose owner has left the screen is open: the largest series on screen in Other takes it at once, and only when none is grey does it wait for its owner. So pods replaced by a deploy hand their colours to the largest pods on screen, a query edit hands the view's colours to the new query's largest series and takes them back on undo, and a pod that misses one result while others are grey comes back in Other.
5. **"Colour the eight largest"** is the person's choice to start the view's map again from what is on screen at that moment: the eight largest series on screen take slots 1 to 8 by rank, the rest on screen are Other, and series hidden or away have no slot afterwards (they are placed by rule 3 when they come on screen). It is offered only when pressing it would change a colour, that is, when one of the eight largest series on screen (ranked as it ranks them) is not coloured: at load, after a narrowing that coloured the eight largest left, and right after it was pressed, it is not offered, because nothing would change (v9: the project lead's decision on the v8 review's m3, "every response meaningful" outranking "offered wherever a series is in Other"). Pressed, it colours every one of the eight largest; it can also move colours among them, since it gives slots by rank. A colour on screen changes only here.

   **Why this rule** (the decision, as recorded). The v6 review found this README and the review rubric giving two verdicts on the same chart; the project lead decided that what a person sees wins (v7). The v7 review found v7 had applied it to filters and not to query edits: a series that left the result kept its slot, so a narrowed query left the screen grey beside unused colours, with nothing offered. The lead's rule: a series not in the result is not on screen either, and every way the screen changes follows one rule (v8). Weighed and not taken: (a) keeping a slot for a series that has left, for its return (v6 and v7: the largest series on screen stay grey beside a colour nobody can see, the picture this rule exists to prevent); (b) lending such a slot only after its owner has been away for more than one result (the v7 review's second fix: two rules for one picture, and a pod that leaves for good keeps its colour away from the screen for a result); (c) ranking hidden series too when the person asks for the eight largest (colours given to series nobody can see). The cost, said plainly: with more than eight series on screen, a series that leaves and comes back (a filter applied and cleared, a query narrowed and widened, a pod that misses one result) comes back in Other; the note says so and "Colour the eight largest" is there. Would change if people mostly narrow or filter to glance away and come straight back; the evidence would be how long filters and narrowed queries stay in recorded sessions.
6. **Everything else is Other**: one entry, drawn as one band from the lowest to the highest folded series at each step (fill `sunken`, edges `series-other`), under the coloured lines, with one legend row. Never a line per folded series, never a legend row per folded series, and never a sum (stacked charts are not part of this component; see "Not specified"). Its row says "Other" and "28 series", and its figures are ranges, "0.1 to 1.5" (one figure when both ends read the same: "< 0.1", never "< 0.1 to < 0.1"; v8), its columns wide enough for a range whose ends are the axis's widest figures or "< 0.1". The rows after it hold what can be done with Other, one action to a row, each a legend row tall (v9: two actions in one row wrapped at 320 px, past the reserved height): "Show 20 more of Other" (then "Hide the 28 in Other") and, when pressing it would change a colour, "Colour the eight largest" (rule 5; v8 offered it whenever Other had members, v7 only when a member of Other was larger than a coloured series).
7. **The note about Other** is one sentence, counted over the series the legend lists, so it never names a colour that is not drawn:
   - if any listed member of Other has a higher maximum in the window than the smallest listed coloured series: "1 in Other is now larger than a coloured series";
   - else "8 coloured by size; 28 in Other".
   (Nothing coloured while Other has members cannot happen: rule 3 gives every open slot to the screen.) With the first sentence one of the eight largest is in Other and "Colour the eight largest" is offered; with the second the eight largest are the coloured series and it is not (the two can differ only where two series have the same highest value).
8. **Picking members of Other.** Opening Other lists its members, 20 at a time, largest first. Choosing one draws it as a line in `ink`; up to three can be drawn at once, the first solid, the second dashed and the third dotted, in the order they were chosen, and each legend swatch shows its pattern. While three are drawn the other members' rows are disabled and say why in their accessible name; choosing a drawn one again removes it.
9. **Colour is never the only channel.** Besides hue: the lightness step, the dash of slots 5 to 8, direct labels where they fit, the readout naming the series nearest the pointer, and **emphasis**: a legend row under the pointer, focused from the keyboard, or held by a finger brings its line forward and draws every other line as a `series-other` hairline until it is let go. Series colours never appear outside a chart and its legend; action and status colours never appear as series. A series that *means* a state (errors, failures) is still a series colour: state is said by the threshold and the status mark, not by drawing a line red.

## Direct labels

- Drawn in a gutter at the right of the plot (`label-gutter` wide), each at the height of its own line's last value, joined to that line end by a leader tick in `ink-muted`. Text is `small` in `ink-secondary`, never the series colour. **The label is one value: the series' most distinguishing label value (the first in the legend's order), cut in the middle to fit the gutter less the leader**, measured in the loaded font (re-measured when a web font arrives). Never several values.
- **Collisions.** Labels are sorted by height and pushed apart to one `small` line height between centres, inside the plot's height. If any label would end more than two line heights from its own line end, or the plot is not tall enough for all of them, **no direct labels are drawn at all** and the legend carries the names. A label on the wrong line is worse than none.
- Drawn only with **two or more** coloured series (one series is named by the panel title), only when every coloured series reaches the right edge, at most eight, never for the Other band and never while members of Other are picked.
- **Below 640px of chart width, and in `data-size="sm"` charts, there are no direct labels**: legend only, and the plot takes the full width.

## Axes, units and numbers

- **Unit.** One display unit per chart, chosen from the largest value drawn (`scaleUnit`): bytes (`B`) in binary units, the largest of B, KiB, MiB, GiB, TiB in which that value is at least 1; seconds and milliseconds in the largest of s, ms, µs in which it is at least 1; any other unit keeps its name, and from 10,000 every number carries the same "k" (from 10,000,000 "M"): "41.2k", unit "req/s". **The unit is written once**, at the top left of the value axis in `ink-secondary`, starting at the chart's left edge and running into the strip (never off the page), and once in the legend's figure head ("Last, MiB").
- **Value axis** (`valueTicks`). It always holds zero, every drawn value and the threshold, and nothing is ever drawn outside the plot. Its span runs from zero, or from the lowest drawn value when that is below zero, to the highest drawn value or the threshold, whichever is higher (a panel may start its axis above zero, stated in its meta, "axis from 99%"; never above its lowest drawn value). The number of intervals is at most the plot height divided by two `micro` lines (32px), and at most four (`plot`: 4; `plot-sm`: 2). The step is the smallest round step (1, 2, 2.5 or 5 times a power of ten, or a power of two for bytes: 128, 256, 512 MiB) whose ticks cover the span in that many intervals; with a threshold, the smallest such step that puts a tick on the threshold, if one does; if none does, the threshold's own label carries its value. The ends are the ticks at or beyond the span: nothing is added for the threshold's label (v5 added 15%, image 06's empty third). Zero is always a tick and its line is `rule-control`; values below zero lie under it (v5 drew them through the time axis and the legend, image 07). Ticks are bare numbers.
- **Numbers.** One format per chart (`numberFormat`). Ticks carry exactly the decimals their step needs (a 2.5 step prints "2.5" and "7.5"). Legend, readout and text form carry **one decimal place more than the place of the step's leading digit**: step 250 or 200, whose leading digit is in the hundreds, prints whole numbers ("312"); step 2.5, 2 or 1 prints tenths ("7.4"); step 0.25 hundredths ("0.12"); never fewer decimals than the ticks and at most three. A value below zero carries a minus sign (U+2212, "−120"). A value that is not zero but would print as zero says which side of zero it is at that precision: "< 0.1" or "> −0.1", never "0.0" and never a sign lost. Figures in the legend are bare; the unit is in the head.
- **Time axis.** The plot's left and right edges are exactly the range's start and end, and both are always labelled with their own time, so the axis never stops short of "now". Between them, ticks at round clock times at least 64px apart (56px over a day); a round tick that would come within 16px of an end label is dropped. 24-hour times; ranges over a day label midnights with the day ("Sat 14") and the ends with the date ("11 Nov 15:16").
- **Step and aggregate** are stated in the panel meta, after the range: "09:16 to 15:16 · 6 min max". If the result was rolled up or sampled, that is where it says so.
- **Gaps are drawn as gaps.** A missing sample breaks the line; nothing is interpolated across it. A sample with a gap on both sides is a dot.
- Gridlines `rule`, the zero line `rule-control`, axis text `micro` in `ink-muted`.

## Thresholds, intervals and markers

- **Threshold**: a dashed `hairline` in `ink-secondary` across the plot (`space-1` dash and gap: shorter and thinner than a series dash), labelled at its left end with its value and what it is: "500 ms objective", "1% threshold", above its line, or under it when above would reach into the strip. A dash with no words is not a threshold. **Which series are above it** (the text form, an agent reading the digest) is computed over every drawn series, the members of Other included: those whose last value is above it ("above it at 15:16"), and those whose highest value in the window is above it but whose last value is not ("above it earlier in the range"). "Above" is strictly greater.
- **Interval** (firing, would have fired, silenced): a `sunken` span the full height of the plot with `rule-control` edges, behind the lines, labelled at its top with what it is and since when or for how long ("Firing since 13:07", "14 min"). The label starts at the span; one too long for the room left ends at the plot's right edge, and if that would make it start before the span, it is cut to its first word ("Firing"), so it never labels time before the span. An interval narrower than one `stroke` is drawn one `stroke` wide, so a 14-minute firing in a 7-day backtest is still visible; the meta says the step.
- **Marker** (deploy, config change, "above threshold since"): a vertical `hairline` in `ink-muted` with its word above the plot, beside its line (to the right, or to the left where the plot ends). Words that would overlap are dropped, later ones first; the lines stay, **the readout names a marker when the crosshair is on it** (within half a step), and every marker is in the text form. A marker an agent placed has the word "Agent:" and the agent's rule (`stroke`, `ink-muted`) before it.
- Words on the plot have a `ground` halo so a line crossing them does not make them unreadable, and every word is drawn after every line.
- **The strip above the plot** holds the unit and the marker words. It is `space-6` tall, and `control-touch` tall with a coarse pointer, so a waiting line's Cancel has its whole hit area over the strip and never over the plot. The plot's top is the strip's height, read from `--strip`, so on a touch screen the taller strip pushes the plot down rather than lying over its top (v5 at first kept the plot `space-6` from the top and a waiting line covered 20px of data on phones). A waiting line lies over it (Panel), starting right of the value axis: it hides marker words while it is shown, never the unit, a tick or data.

## Readout, crosshair, zoom

- **Crosshair**: a vertical `hairline` in `ink`, snapped to a sample, with a dot on each drawn line and an `ink` ring round the dot of the series the readout names. It moves without laying anything out again.
- **The readout never covers the plot.** It is one line in the foot, left of the zoom buttons, at every width (its height is the foot's, reserved; there is no separate readout line). It holds the time, the marker under the crosshair if any, and one or two named values: with a pointer or a finger on the plot, the series **nearest** it; and the **highest** drawn series, when that is a different series and the chart is at least 640px wide. From the keyboard, the highest. "Drawn" means every line and every member of the Other band, so when the highest is in Other the readout says "highest: Other, checkout-api-…new01 3.6 cores". Names are cut in the middle to fit. Each series' value replaces the legend's figure column, whose head becomes "At 13:02, ms".
- **Pointer**: move to read; drag across the plot to select a range (a `sunken` span with `ink` edges), release to zoom to it.
- **Touch**: drag horizontally to move the crosshair (the plot allows vertical page scroll only). Zoom is the two buttons in the foot. A legend tap toggles a series; holding a finger on a row brings its line forward; there is no modifier key anywhere.
- **Zoom**: "Zoom in" halves the range and "Zoom out" doubles it, centred on the crosshair when it is set; **without a crosshair the end of the range stays where it is**, so a range that ends now still ends now. Never past the data; Zoom out is disabled at the whole range, Zoom in below four samples. Plus and minus do the same from the keyboard.
- **Keyboard**: the plot is one tab stop. Left and Right move the crosshair one sample (ten with Shift), Home and End go to the ends, Escape clears it, plus and minus zoom.
- **Screen readers**: the plot's accessible name says what the chart is, its unit, range, how many series and how they are drawn. The readout is announced by a separate polite live region: at once after a keyboard move, and after the pointer has rested 500 ms, never on every pointer move.

## Legend

- A table of series: swatch and name, then Last, Min, Max, with the unit once in the head ("Last, ms"). Min and Max are priority 2 and are dropped below 640px of table width; Name and Last stay. Figure columns are as wide as the widest figure they can show, so moving the crosshair never moves a column.
- Heads are sortable (`vz-sort`); each fills its head, with `space-1` less the head's rule above and below its words, so its focus ring, drawn inside (the legend clips), keeps a hairline clear of them (v9). It opens in slot order. Sorting never repaints. **Sorting uses the window's own values** (Last, Min, Max of the range shown), never the values at the crosshair, so rows never move while someone reads. While the crosshair is set, the figure column shows the values at the crosshair, its head reads "At 13:02, ms" and carries no triangle, and if the rows are sorted by Last the name head says so: "Series (pod · node), by last value". Pressing the figure head while the crosshair is set clears the crosshair and sorts by Last.
- **Each row is a toggle.** Choosing rows draws only the chosen series; choosing none draws all. Chosen rows are `ink` strong on `rule` (`aria-selected`), the rest are `ink-muted` with a hollow swatch (`data-off`). A press shows at once (`:active`). The focus ring is round the whole row.
- The legend is one tab stop; Up and Down move between rows; Space or Enter toggles; focus on a row brings its line forward.
- **Names: distinguishing labels first.** (1) Drop every label that has the same value on all series, and state those once in the legend note ("All series: namespace=\"prod\""). (2) Order the remaining labels by how many distinct values they take, most first; ties by name. The head names them in that order: "Series (pod · node · service)". (3) Show the values in that order, separated by " · ". (4) Every row shows the same labels: if any row's name is too wide, the last label is dropped from all rows (and from the head), until all fit or one label is left; a single value that is still too wide is cut **in the middle**, keeping its start and its end. The full label set is the row's title and its accessible name.
- **The note** is one line per fact, each cut to fit, so its height is known before the data arrives: the shared labels ("All series: …", cut in the middle), and for Other either how the colours were chosen ("8 coloured by size; 28 in Other") or that Other now holds something larger (rule 7).
- A single-series chart keeps the legend row: it carries the figures.

## Text form

Every chart has a text form, the same facts in sentences, used for the "Text" view, the digest a screen reader can ask for, and export. It is computed by `textLines` from the same model and facts (`chartModel`, `drawnSet`, `chartFacts`) as the plot, the legend and the readout, so the three cannot disagree. In order: the title, unit, range, step and aggregate, and how many series and how they are drawn ("Latency by route, p99, ms, 09:16 to 15:16, every 6 min, max. 3 series."), with how many are drawn when legend rows are chosen; the labels every series shares; one line per drawn coloured series with its name (distinguishing labels), last, lowest and highest, and whether part of the range has no data; each member of Other picked out, with its pattern; Other, with how many series, the range of their last values and its highest member by name ("the highest, checkout-api-…new01, reached 4.0"); the note about Other, word for word; the threshold and which drawn series are above it at the end and earlier (members of Other named "Other, …"); each interval with its times; each marker. After an error, a first line says the latest query failed and that what follows is the previous query's result. Numbers use the chart's number format. The preview's routes, pods, limit and change charts show it.

## Text and size

- Chart text is `micro` (axes, words on the plot) and `small` (direct labels, legend, foot), drawn at device pixels. The chart is drawn at the size of its region and redrawn when that changes; it is never scaled like an image, at any width or zoom.
- Plot height is `plot` in a dashboard panel, `plot-sm` beside a form or a claim.
- **Reserved height.** Every chart reserves its plot, strip, foot and legend head before its data arrives, and the rows and note lines of its legend: it sets `--legend-rows` (every row the legend draws: the coloured series and, when there is an Other, its row, the members shown and one row per action) and `--legend-notes` (0 to 2) on the `vz-chart`, inline or from a stylesheet, **in its markup**, from its last result, so the first frame of a load is already the height it will draw (v9: the Chart preview's, the reference's and the charts composition's charts set neither, reserved 265 px against up to 1,059 drawn, and a reload scrolled to one of them moved the page by up to 1.69; the v8 review's B1). It then reserves its whole height from the same custom properties its parts are drawn with (`--chart-plot`, `--strip`, `--chart-foot`, `--legend-head`, `--legend-row`), which are the drawn heights and which the coarse-pointer rules set to touch sizes. **The reference chart sets both again whenever it draws its legend** (a new result, "Show 20 more of Other", a recolour), so its reservation stays what it draws as the legend changes, including while it is skipped off screen. Reserved and drawn are equal to the pixel, with either pointer and at every width: nothing in the chart wraps (the legend's actions have a row each). Checked as rendered: `check.mjs` reads every chart of the previews in its markup (the page with no script run), once drawn, and after "Show 20 more of Other", at 1200 px fine and 400 and 320 px coarse, and fails a chart with no reservation; `tools/audit.mjs` reads every chart on every page at every configuration (`reserve`) and reloads every page with charts scrolled to each chart (`reload-shift`; v5 reserved one hairline more per legend row than it drew).

## States

**Off screen** (v8): a chart is `content-visibility: auto`. Far from the viewport it is neither styled, laid out nor painted, so a change of theme (or anything else that restyles the page) costs what is on screen, not every chart on it. While skipped it is its reserved height (its `min-height`, the same calculation as what it draws), and once drawn it keeps the size it last had; so it is skipped without moving anything only because its reservation is exact (v9: in v8 most charts on the system's own pages reserved less than they drew, and a page that loaded scrolled past them landed elsewhere; the v8 review's B1). A rule that undoes the skipping for some charts fails `check.mjs` and the audit's `skip`. Its paint is contained: anything drawn outside its box is clipped at `space-3` around it (`overflow-clip-margin`), more than a ring or a small control's 44 px hit area reaches, and nothing in a chart floats outside it (no menu, no tooltip). Put a menu or a popover outside the chart, never inside it.

Loading and stale: see Panel. Empty: `vz-empty` in the plot's place, at the plot's height. **Error after a result was drawn** (`setError`, `data-error` on the chart): the message, with the critical mark and what to change, lies over the plot at its height (the plot stays laid out under it, hidden, so nothing moves and the next result draws at once); the readout is cleared and zoom hidden; the legend keeps the previous result's names and figures and is inert (there is no plot for its rows to change), and its swatches are hollow, because no line is drawn for them (v5 review, m3). So the head's "Showing the previous query" and its age describe what is still on screen, and the reserved height holds with true content, not an empty space. An error with nothing drawn yet is `vz-empty` in the plot's place, and the head states no age. Partial: the meta says what is missing. Crosshair, range selection, chosen rows, sorted head, expanded Other, picked members, emphasis, a filter round trip with a new series arriving meanwhile, a query edited and edited back, a member of Other above a threshold, values below zero, bytes, a backtest and the error state are all in the preview.

## Not specified

Stacked charts, bar charts and heatmaps are not part of this component. Do not build them from it; each needs its own rules (a stack's Other is a sum, which this component never draws). A trace's spans are Waterfall and log lines are Log, each its own component.
