# Spike S10: traces and logs on Cloudflare, both flags on

The experiment `deploy/README.md` names under "From stage 3 of traces and logs" ("A trial on
Cloudflare"), and that the adversarial review `design/reviews/signals-end-to-end.md`, D62 ("Would
change if") and D71 ("Would change if") ask for: a staged deployment with `INGEST_SIGNALS` and
`QUERY_SIGNALS` on, OTLP traces, OTLP logs and Loki push through the gateway at realistic batch
sizes, the flush, ledger, compaction and publication of span and log files against real R2, and
Tempo and Loki queries back, measured on Cloudflare.

## Ingest

**Generator defect found first** (affects `edge/bench/bodies.mjs`, not viz): its LCG computes
`s * 1103515245` in doubles, which passes 2^53 and loses bits, so it falls into short cycles: a
"realistic" body of 8,200 spans has only **267** (seed 7) to **520** distinct (trace id, span id)
pairs (`check-bodies.mjs` prints them; measured here). Edge decodes and the shards store all 8,200
items, so the ingest CPU and buffer numbers of run 1 stand; but flush keeps one row per key, so
run 1's files and queries see about 5% of what was acknowledged. Run 2 uses the same generator
computed exactly (`lcg32`: `Math.imul`, every span new).

- Every refusal was **429 "ingest buffer is full; retry the whole request"**; no 5xx, no other status.
  In run 1 the two span shards took 28 requests in the first 5.4 s (about 100 MB decoded, the tenant's
  64 MiB per shard), then refused everything for about 33 s while flush drained. Even paced at about
  2.5 requests a second (about 20,000 spans/s, 9 MB/s decoded) the traces buffer filled. **Sustained
  per-tenant trace ingest on this stage is below about 2.5 requests/s of 8,200 spans** (measured);
  Loki at 8,200 entries was never refused at the rates tried.
- **A 429 costs edge a full decode** (tail): traces refused p50 198 ms of CPU, accepted 204 ms; JSON
  332 vs 329 ms. Edge refuses before decoding only when a shard sheds load (`refuseEarly`), not when the
  tenant's buffer is full, so a sender retrying under backpressure spends a decode per attempt
  (run 1's 132 refused trace attempts: about 26 s of CPU).
- **A request refused at the end can be partly stored.** A tenant's spans go to two shards; when one
  appends and the other is full the request is answered 429 as a whole. After run 1, every one of 30
  sampled accepted traces was found by ID, and so were 4 of 10 traces that appear only in requests
  that were finally refused; the stored distinct spans (5,946, `count_over_time`) lie between those
  of the accepted requests (5,524) and of everything generated (6,547). Nothing acknowledged was
  missing; a sender that retries gets each batch once (the shards' fingerprints), one that gives up
  leaves part of its request behind.

**Where CPU is counted** (found here; Cloudflare's pricing page, read 2026-11-19: a service-binding
call is "one request" with "the total amount of CPU time used across both Worker A and Worker B"):
when a chain starts in a Durable Object (the load clients), the analytics API counts the gateway's and
edge's invocations under the object's namespace (`Client`: 3,710 "invocations" for a few hundred
operations), and viz-s10-ingest and viz-s10-query have no fetch rows of their own. When the chain starts
at an internet request, each Worker of it has its own rows. That is why per-Worker CPU comes from the
tail for the load runs and from analytics for the requests sent from outside.

## The D71 limits on Cloudflare

All 35 bodies of `edge/bench/memory.mjs --dump` (the review's hostile shapes and bodies.mjs's realistic
ones), sent once each through the gateway (client, 14:10:12–14:10:31) and again one at a time from
outside (14:41–14:44), gave **the same answer as edge's decoders on this machine for all 35** (status,
rows and refused counts; `node bench/memory.mjs --node --json` in the worktree): 413 for every JSON body
over 2^20 values (4 bodies) and for the metrics resource fan-out (16 MiB decoded); 400 for the JSON of
empty arrays and for the Loki entry of 65,536+ metadata values; 200 with `partial_success` naming the
one refused item (65,536+ values in a span, a record, a body array; a span of 129+ events; a 4 MiB span
name) or the 2,097,152 refused empty spans and records. A 9 MiB body and a gzip body inflating to 9 MiB
were 413. No invocation failed or exceeded a limit (tail and analytics).

CPU per hostile body (analytics, outside): 28 ms (4 MiB span name) to **3,401 ms** (4 MiB of empty
spans, 2,097,152 refused one by one; about 4 KB gzipped), 2,298 ms (empty records), 1,938 ms (200,000
minimal records), 1,861 ms (107,546 minimal spans), 1,127 ms (Loki JSON small entries), 1,095 ms (the
metrics fan-out, 413). The same empty-spans body took about 1.4 s in a warm isolate (tail).
Isolate memory per hostile request: at most **78.5 MiB** (traces resource attribute array), every one
under 128 MB.

## Flush, ledger, compaction, publication

- **Acknowledgement → queryable** (client, s10-q, three runs of 20 traces and 20 Loki lines, 13:58:19–
  13:58:31): **every one of the 120 answered by the first query after its acknowledgement.** Traces:
  ack p50 32–43 ms, ack → answered by trace by ID p50 35–49 ms, p99 at most 203 ms; lines: ack
  p50 32–39 ms, ack → answered p50 26–41 ms. (The same experiment locally in workerd: 3–6 ms,
  `stack/README.md`.) The first request of a new tenant: ack 467 ms, answered 1,303 ms after.
- **Acknowledgement → served from a file** (three of those traces asked every 20 s until the tail was
  empty and a file answered): **577.5, 598.5 and 599.0 s**: the 10-minute age seal, then the queue and
  a flush. Under load segments seal by size; that path was measured by its parts: flush messages waited
  **7–58 s** in the queue (analytics `lagTime`, per-minute average), consumer concurrency scaled to 6.8–14
  at peaks, and a flush invocation took up to 16.6 s of wall time (tail; p90 1.3–7 s) with at most
  2,169 ms of CPU. The consumer flushes the (tenant, shard) groups of a batch one after another
  (`flush/src/consumer.ts`), so a batch's wall time is the sum.
- **Flush units** (R2 listing, s10-load2, distinct ids): one 4 MiB segment of spans became a `spans`
  file of **723 KB** (p50 of 68) and a `trace_index` file of 287 KB; a logs segment a `logs` file of
  950 KB (p50 of 48). About **69 bytes of `spans` and 27 of `trace_index` per span**. These bodies put
  every span in its own trace, the worst case for `trace_index`; s10-q, at 12 spans a trace, has 2% of
  its span bytes in `trace_index`.
- **Ledger** (analytics): `addUnit` wall p50 about 47 ms (tail); ledger RPCs 4,009 at CPU p50 0.6 ms,
  wall p50 32 ms; alarms (the log drain) 371, wall p50 388 ms. R2 `PutObject` p50 335 ms, p90 468, p99
  962 (n = 1,358); `GetObject` p50 207, p99 330 (n = 1,301).
- **Compaction ran on Cloudflare**, at both levels: open hours to `L1-hHH`, closed days to `L2-d`, for
  all three tables (R2 listing). **CPU is the problem.** One merge of realistic logs took 2.8 to
  10.2 s of CPU; maintenance runs that did 8 merges (the per-run maximum, `more` left) took **20,962 ms
  and 28,449 ms of CPU** (39 and 52 s of wall time; tail, 14:26:56 and 14:30:37). On **2026-11-20 at
  02:07:24 UTC**, when the 19th closed, the closed-day run **was killed at the CPU limit** (analytics:
  `exceededResources`, 32,000 ms of CPU, 72 s wall, 108 MiB isolate); the queue retried it 73 s later and
  it succeeded with **22,780 ms of CPU, 100.3 MiB of WASM memory and 158.1 MiB reported isolate memory**
  (analytics, as reported; above the 128 MB documented for an isolate, not explained here). Nothing
  was lost (the merge is a `replace`; no dead letter), but one more input would have failed it again.
- **Publication** (R2 listing, tail): the publisher committed `spans` and `trace_index` 7 times and
  `logs` 8 times by 14:32 (15, 15 and 16 metadata versions by 2026-11-26, as maintenance went on),
  the first at **14:01:04**, about 2.5 minutes after the first span's acknowledgement (13:58:2x): the
  2-minute nudge delay plus the commit. Alarms: CPU p50 56.5 ms, p99 139 ms; wall p50 6.7 s, p99 11.4 s
  (analytics, 15 alarms). Twice (14:01:46, 14:01:51) a maintenance run "could not ask the publisher …
  within 2000 ms" while it was publishing, and deleted nothing that run, as designed.
- **Read back by readers viz did not write** (`readback.py`, 14:40, of the latest metadata): PyIceberg
  0.12.0 planned and read each tenant through the metadata, manifest list and manifests (partition
  `tenant`); pyarrow and DuckDB 2.0.0 read the same files. All three agree file by file, and the rows
  are **exactly what was acknowledged**: s10-q 47,402 spans and 75,061 log rows, s10-fat 3,000 spans and
  440 lines, s10-fatattr 600 spans; `trace_index` 4,502, 3,000 and 50 rows (fragments, one per trace and
  file).

## What it means

- **D57** (per-signal pools): held on Cloudflare. Tails by signal, header checks and the metrics read
  on a tenant with span and log streams all answered right. Edge's decode on Cloudflare is 1.3 to
  2.5 times this machine's Node when warm, up to about 6 times on a cold isolate. Two things not in
  D57: a 429 after one of a tenant's two shards appended leaves that half stored, and a 429 is decided
  only after a full decode.
- **D62** (the query budget): **the memory budget holds**: every fat shape refused cleanly with the
  local numbers (4.8 and 6.0 MiB; 422 at 40.7 and 44.5 MiB), the realistic near-cap search at
  31–34 MiB of WASM, query isolates at most 70.8 MiB, no `exceededMemory`. **The caps make trace queries
  unusable at realistic volume**: at about 690,000 spans in an hour every trace query, trace by ID
  included, is refused, and a smaller margin does not help. Its "would change if Cloudflare shows the
  margin's reads cost too much" is answered in an unexpected way: the reads are cheap where they fit;
  what fails is `maxFetchBytes` against a single hour, which only split execution, a trace-id-sorted
  `trace_index` with per-trace pruning of `spans`, or larger isolates would change. Also: the
  `max_log_rows` pre-check counts whole row groups, so logs sent together but far apart in time refuse
  each other's queries.
- **D63** (the write side): flush of span and log segments against real R2 works and admits units
  (`addUnit` about 47 ms); but **flush throughput bounds ingest**: queue lag up to 58 s, groups of a
  batch flushed one after another, and the tenant's buffer full at about 2.5 requests/s of 8,200
  spans. Retention was not exercised.
- **D67** (publication): worked on Cloudflare: commits within 2–4 minutes of a flush, alarms at most
  139 ms of CPU, and three independent readers read exactly the acknowledged rows. The upgrade's
  bounded re-bases (M-6) were not exercised.
- **D68** (compaction): works, at both levels, but its CPU model is off: about 2.6–3.6 s of CPU per
  merge and 8 merges a run reach 21–28 s against the 30 s default, and one closed-day run **was killed
  at the limit** (retried successfully), with 100 MiB of WASM memory against the 62–81 MB modelled.
  Its "would change if" applies now: bound merges per run by CPU spent (or by measured cost), or raise
  `limits.cpu_ms`, or move merges to a Container.
- **D71** (decode limits): **confirmed on Cloudflare**: 35 of 35 bodies answered as locally; the
  isolate stayed at 16–78.5 MiB per request. What D71 did not model: CPU. A 4 KB gzip body of 4 MiB of
  empty spans costs 1.4–3.4 s of CPU; at the 8 MiB limit about twice that, under the 30 s limit but a
  large amplification for any key with `write`.
- **`QUERY_SIGNALS`** should **not** go on yet for tenants beyond small volumes: trace queries are
  refused above about a quarter of a million spans in an hour. It could go on for small tenants (the
  measured s10-q shapes answered within 0.3–2 s, inside budget); the second review of D62 and a Grafana
  run are still outstanding. `INGEST_SIGNALS` worked end to end, with D63's throughput and D68's CPU as
  the risks.
